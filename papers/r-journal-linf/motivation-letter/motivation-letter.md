Please consider the article "Linf: L-Infinity Normalization and Dominant
Community State Types in R" for publication in The R Journal.

The article describes the `linf` package, which provides a dependency-light R
implementation of L-infinity normalization, dominant-feature assignment,
hierarchical dominant Community State Types, frozen-hierarchy transfer,
representative landmarks, and zero-aware homogeneous-coordinate embeddings.
The package is available on CRAN, includes unit tests and workflow vignettes,
and supports both dense and sparse matrices.

The manuscript goes beyond the package documentation by explaining the design
of the fitted objects and policy views, distinguishing sample-wise dominance
from cohort-fitted state vocabularies, demonstrating transfer to held-out
samples, comparing the package with neighboring compositional-data tools, and
reporting a reproducible dense-versus-sparse benchmark. Two bundled microbiome
datasets provide fully executable examples, while the limitations section
states where dCST summaries should not replace continuous or inferential
analyses.

All reported results, tables, and figures are generated from R code during the
article build. The complete build runs in less than ten minutes and the
submission includes the source, bibliography, package list, motivating letter,
and reproducibility code.
